This SQL project explores wealth distribution among the world's 200 richest billionaires. It examines their net worth, age, geographical distribution and industries to uncover patterns and provide insights into global wealth concentration.

# Business Scenario
You have been employed as a Junior Data Analyst by a global financial research organisation. The organisation wants to understand wealth distribution among the world's 200 richest people.

# Project Objectives
- Identify the 20 richest billionaires.
- Examine the age distribution of billionaires.
- Determine the most represented countries.
- Analyse the distribution of wealth across countries.
- Identify industries with the highest number of billionaires.
- Investigate the relationship between age and wealth.
- Identify the industry with the highest average net worth.

# Dataset Overview
The Top 200 Richest World's Billionaires Dataset provides a detailed overview of the wealthiest 
individuals globally, according to the Forbes list. This dataset captures essential information 
about the top 200 billionaires, offering insights into their financial standing, age, industry, and 
more. It is a valuable resource for analyzing trends in wealth distribution, industry dominance, 
and the demographics of the world's richest people.

# Key Insights and Findings

- The top five billionaires have a combined net worth of $940 billion, demonstrating the significant concentration of wealth among a relatively small number of individuals.
  
- The youngest billionaires are predominantly associated with technology and business enterprises. This suggests that entrepreneurship and ownership of scalable businesses can create substantial wealth at relatively young ages.
  
- The average age of billionaires in the dataset is approximately 69 years, while their average net worth is approximately $31.84 billion.
This suggests that substantial wealth among the world's richest individuals is not limited to younger entrepreneurs but also includes individuals who have accumulated wealth over several decades.

- The United States accounts for 39.5% of all billionaires in the dataset. This indicates a significant concentration of billionaire wealth holders in the country compared with other nations.
  
- The United States accounts for approximately 49.2% of the combined wealth represented in the dataset.
Interestingly, France ranks second in combined net worth despite having only seven billionaires, compared with China's 17.
This demonstrates that the number of billionaires in a country does not necessarily determine its total billionaire wealth.

- Technology leads with 40 billionaires, representing 20% of the dataset.
Finance & Investments and Fashion & Retail follow closely.
This suggests that technology, financial services and retail are significant sectors among the world's wealthiest individuals.

- The United States has a considerably higher number of billionaires than every other country in the dataset.
Only eight countries meet the minimum threshold of five billionaires, highlighting the uneven geographical distribution of billionaire representation.

- Although individuals below 50 represent only 8% of the dataset, they collectively hold $508.3 billion in wealth.
This demonstrates that substantial wealth creation is possible at relatively young ages.

- Only 26.5% of the billionaires have wealth above the overall average.
This suggests that wealth is not evenly distributed even within the group of the world's 200 richest individuals.

- Although Telecom has the highest average net worth, its result is based on just one individual. Technology provides a more representative comparison because it has the largest number of billionaires.
